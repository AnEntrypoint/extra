The bench behind every build: the tools that make the models, pictures, sounds, tests and helpers that go into a game. Open one to see how it works.

#### The workshop at a glance

The making bench behind every build: Blender models, painted pictures, sound and voices, uploaded models, video, playtests and helper wisps, and when each is reached for.

read →

#### Blender: hero models

Blender builds the pieces that need real shape: a statue with centuries eaten off its rim, a sagging rope bridge, a creature or a rigged character, each from one re-runnable script.

read →

#### Painting pictures

Painted pictures in seconds: icons, posters, portraits, mock screens, wall paintings, seamless textures and cutouts, each a picture address the game shows.

read →

#### Sound, music & voices

Sounds, music tracks, songs and voice lines are named into being: the filename is the prompt. How they are made, placed, mixed and kept from getting too loud.

read →

#### Uploaded & built models

A .glb you upload or a wisp exports arrives with a spec sheet, then gets fitted and dressed: textures on named slots, a lantern in a fist, a cape that moves, sockets and swappable parts.

read →

#### Video, trailers & clips

Trailers filmed from real play, short scenes made from a sentence or a still, looping backdrops and speaking portraits, and where each one plays in your game.

read →

#### Testers, eyes & playtests

How your helpers see and play what they build: camera looks at the world, a body that plays your game in a real browser page, tester players in their own room, and live rounds.

read →

#### Wisps & weaves

Wisps are helpers that build pieces of your world at the same time. A weave plans them: a brief each, a lane each, then reviewers, critics and players to check the work.

read →

#### World files, versions & undo

Your world is a folder of files, and every save is a version. Read the history, film old versions, undo a change whole, and keep several builders from stepping on each other.

read →

#### Platform reads

What your helper can read about your game as players meet it: errors and render faults from their devices, frame rate and performance tables, bug reports, and the publish and launch steps.
