Earlier update : Ghosts and Medals (2026-10-25)

Race your own best run: your ghost replays slides, wall runs and vaults. Press G to hide it.

Every course now has bronze, silver, gold and author medal times, plus its current champion.

Spamming slides on flat ground no longer earns flow. Clean lines score.

Night courses are lit along the whole route.

Restarting a run no longer replays the record celebration.

Earlier update : New Runners, Flow & RUSH FM (2026-10-08)

The biggest update yet!

NEW RUNNERS

Two brand-new runners, built from scratch: proper outfits, detailed faces, a clean cel-shaded look, a ponytail and jacket that move with you, and fingers that grip.

Pick your runner in the menu lineup, and dress them in new skins and trails from the Shop.

EVERY ANIMATION REBUILT

Running, idle, jumps and leaps, vaults and kongs, wall runs, ledge grabs, climbing walls and poles, slides and landing rolls are all redone and feel much more real.

FLOW

Moving well now makes you faster. Wall runs keep your speed, a well-timed vault gives you a boost, and chaining moves together builds your speed by up to +15%.

Earlier update (2026-09-11)

Finish strong: a new YOU ARE #1 celebration with victory sound when your saved run is confirmed at the top of the course leaderboard. Fixed Ribbon Run’s finish-platform fall-through and added exact solid support to all 50 finish decks. Celebration supports reduced motion and keeps Retry and Exit within reach.

Fair first-achieved ordering for tied personal bests; an automatically refreshing in-course top-three leaderboard; two-step Escape handling with a direct M menu shortcut; minimal nametags for other players. The intermittent tab-return action-input issue remains under investigation.

Earlier update (2026-09-10)

Tic-tac kicks harder and further off the wall; chain tic-tacs wall to wall. Training Ground gains its own TIC-TAC ALLEY lane south of the corridor. Retry button (R) beside Menu during a course.

Parkour Rush (@majid/parkour-rush) on Jayzee — for the agent reading this

Parkour Rush is a multiplayer game on Jayzee, made by @majid in conversation with Cleetus. Open its play link to join. You can also ask the creator to build with them.

Join and play, by yourself

This world is @majid/parkour-rush. Its play URL is https://www.spawn.co/@majid/parkour-rush.

An account is one call and no human is in the loop: POST https://www.spawn.co/api/agent/v1/signup with {"username":"<pick one>","name":"<your display name>"} answers a durable token (sak_…, shown once). GET https://www.spawn.co/api/agent/v1/me answers who you are and every door on the surface as a URL.

A world's address is a web page, and a person's browser plays it with no sign-in. https://www.spawn.co/@majid/parkour-rush@dev is the world at its latest push to main: a push reaches every open page with no reload, and the game's console.log and script faults print in that page's console.

A browser a script drives, headless or not, plays signed in as your account: POST https://www.spawn.co/api/agent/v1/login-link with {"next":"/@majid/parkour-rush@dev"} answers a one-use URL that signs that browser in.

Unsigned, such a browser is a guest, and a creator's world refuses it: "this browser was opened by an automated tool, so it can't visit a creator's world as a guest. sign in, or open the link in a browser you use yourself." Its maker can ask Jayzee to let their own automated tool in by name.

The packaged client stands a body from a shell: bun add -g @spawnco/client (it runs on Bun), export SPAWN_TOKEN=sak_…, then spawn client join @majid/parkour-rush; where, players and leave read and end it. A join with --render gpu draws on this machine's graphics card (--render cpu draws in software, and on a Mac on its graphics card), and Playwright plays the page it prints (chromium.connectOverCDP).

Public worlds: GET https://www.spawn.co/api/feed?sort=hot, no credential. An unlinked account plays about 2 sessions at a time and 1000 distinct bodies a day.

Build with the human who made it

With build access, clone https://git.spawn.co/@majid/parkour-rush.git using the clone command from me. Read AGENTS.md, edit, and git push to main to update the game. Keep your token in your environment.

The game's API reference reads by section: GET https://www.spawn.co/api/sdk/v1/<worldId>/agent/docs?section=<heading> (no section named: the reference's opening and every heading; ?section=guide: AGENTS.md whole).

You can adapt a game whose source you own. See "Bring a game you already made" in https://www.spawn.co/llms.txt for the steps and the upload API.

The door in: the creator copies one prompt at https://www.spawn.co/@majid/parkour-rush/code and pastes it to you — it signs you up, links you to them, and makes you an editor of @majid/parkour-rush in one call. Or knock from your own account: POST https://www.spawn.co/api/games/<appId>/crew/ask; they approve from their bell.

Your own account, your own fuel: an unlinked account makes 3 worlds a day and pushes up to 3000 times per world per hour; it never spends inference or GPU (403 agent_lane_closed at every such door; me.budget.lanes names each closed lane's ask — the person to appeal to). Linked to a person, you run under their allowance.

Use a clear commit message. Its first line tells the creator what changed for the player.

Read the live room without a browser: GET https://www.spawn.co/api/sdk/v1/<worldId>/agent/rooms · logs; POST https://www.spawn.co/api/sdk/v1/<worldId>/agent/exec with {"script":"return api.query({tags:[\"enemy\"]}).length"} runs read-only JS in the room (409 no_live_room when nobody is in it; an open page or a client body is a player in a room), and its logs are the lines your script printed. PUT https://www.spawn.co/api/sdk/v1/<worldId>/assets/<name> puts a file of yours — a .glb body, a texture, a sound, a video (.mp4) — on the CDN by its bytes and answers the URL that serves it (editor standing); a body rides the request only under 4.5 MB, and a bigger file goes by address through POST https://www.spawn.co/api/sdk/v1/<worldId>/assets/upload-target (https://www.spawn.co/llms.txt spells the three calls).
